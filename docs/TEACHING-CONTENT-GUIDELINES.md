# Teaching Content Guidelines

InGauge teaches powerful ideas in a simple, safe, and engaging way — **psychology without the jargon**. Use these guidelines when writing or editing Manual lessons, tool copy, or any content that explains concepts.

## 1. Explain Like a Human (3 layers)

- **Layer 1 — Simple sentence:** One short explanation anyone can understand.  
  Example: *"A trigger is something that causes a strong emotional reaction in you."*
- **Layer 2 — Real-life example:** One concrete example.  
  Example: *"If someone ignores your message, it might trigger feelings of rejection."*
- **Layer 3 — Optional deeper:** Only if the user wants more (e.g. "Learn more" / "Want to go deeper?"). Don’t dump everything at once.

## 2. Micro-learning

- Aim for **20–40 seconds** of reading per concept.
- **One concept per lesson or card.** Let it land before adding more.
- Prefer **visual + short text** where possible (e.g. emotion wheel, sliders).

## 3. Story over definition

- Prefer a **story or scenario** over a textbook definition.
- Example: Instead of *"Avoidant attachment means someone struggles with emotional closeness,"* use: *"Some people really care about their partner but pull away when things get serious. That often comes from learning early that depending on others wasn’t safe."*

## 4. Plain language

- No clinical or psychology jargon unless the user uses it first.
- Translate terms: e.g. *"Cognitive dissonance is the uncomfortable feeling you get when your actions don’t match what you believe."*

## 5. "You’re not broken" messaging

- Normalize: *"Lots of people experience this."* *"You’re not alone."*
- Frame as **skills and understanding**, not disorders or labels.
- Avoid language that makes users feel defective.

## 6. Safety for young users

- Calm, supportive tone. No judgment, no lecturing.
- Sensitive questions: answer in an **educational, respectful** way. Focus on understanding, not moralizing.
- **Never diagnose or label.** Focus on what they can do and how things work.

## 7. Transparency (when useful)

- You can say *"This comes from research in psychology"* or *"Research shows…"* without overloading with citations. Builds trust.

## 8. One moment of clarity

- Every explanation should leave the user thinking: **"Oh… that makes sense."**
- Goal: **clarity, not confusion.**
