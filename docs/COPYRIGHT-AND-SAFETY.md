# InGauge — Copyright & Safety

Guidance so the app stays clear of copyright issues and legal risk.

## Copyright & content

- **Original content:** Manual, discoveries, tool copy, and in-app text should be written by you or licensed for use. Do not paste in long excerpts from books, papers, or other apps.
- **Attribution:** Where you reference or adapt a specific model or framework (e.g. cognitive distortions, attachment styles), add a short in-app or settings attribution (e.g. “Based on …”) and keep wording your own.
- **22+ books / research:** The app is “grounded in” research and lived experience; describe it that way. Do not reproduce copyrighted material; summarize and cite at a high level (e.g. “Research shows…” with a generic or your own phrasing).
- **AI-generated copy:** If you use AI to draft in-app text, ensure the final copy is reviewed and that you have rights to publish it (e.g. terms of the AI provider and your own policy).

## Safety & disclaimers

- **Not medical/therapy:** InGauge is not a medical device, not a replacement for therapy or professional care. Say this clearly in onboarding and in Settings (e.g. “What InGauge Is / Is Not”).
- **Crisis:** Always provide 988, Crisis Text Line (741741), 911, and a clear disclaimer that the app is not a substitute for professional help.
- **No diagnose/treat:** Avoid language that implies diagnosis, treatment, or cure. Use “observe,” “reflect,” “support,” “insights,” “patterns” instead.
- **Age:** If the app is for 13+, state that and design content appropriately. Do not make unsupported health claims for any age group.

## Trademarks

- **InGauge / PHOSM:** Use as your own product name and framework name.
- **Third-party:** Do not use other companies’ trademarks in a way that suggests endorsement (e.g. “Oura,” “Apple Health” only to describe integration, not to imply they endorse the app).

Keeping content original or properly attributed, and disclaimers clear, helps avoid copyright and legal trouble.
